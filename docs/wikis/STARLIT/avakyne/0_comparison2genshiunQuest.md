## Description

The protagonist often wants a dream world where everything is perfect. There is no struggle or difference. The problem is that when there is no struggle, there is no fulfilment because everything is the same. This is never said outright in any of the games, but is a central theme; the conflict always involves hero's eventual fight against Avakyne who initially helps the hero's own desire for a world without conflict.


Avakyne is not manipulative, cunning or calculating. Often, she will genuinely help the main character. As she describes, watching mortals dance about is an interesting way to pass time. In fact, she often frequents these worlds to try their snacks ('entertainment' or 'leisure' activity) even though she knows she intends to destroy it. She's convinced herself that mortals are mere playthings. In truth, she genuinely wants to make a better timeline, but she herself doesn't admit to this or realize. This is the thing that drives her attitude, her decision to help protagonists and ultimately her decision to destroy the world when the timeline becomes too chaotic and full of grief.


Avakyne is supremely powerful and a bit cocky. She has a "that's just the way it is" attitude concerning her own powers and inventions. She also often replies "why not" or "why can't you" when confronting challenges mortals think are "impossible", such as it's impossible to turn back time when facing a regret.


Avakyne is a dynamic character, but her story is cross universal and far away.
